You reserve enough cash to buy 100 shares at the strike and collect premium for taking that obligation.

  • Use only on a stock you are willing to own.
  • Set the strike at an acceptable purchase price.
  • Keep the full assignment cash reserved.

You pay a debit for long-dated upside — typically more than one year — without owning 100 shares.

  • Use for a bullish, long-term thesis.
  • Risk only premium you can lose completely.
  • Allow for time decay and an expiration deadline.

Long vs. short options
"Long" means you bought the contract; "short" means you sold it. The words describe the option position — not your market outlook by themselves.
  • Long option (pay a debit): you own a right. A LEAPS call gives you the right, not the obligation, to buy shares at the strike before expiration. Maximum loss is the premium paid. Time works against the buyer as expiration approaches.
  • Short option (collect a credit): you accept an obligation. A covered call is backed by shares; a cash-secured put is backed by the cash needed for assignment. Maximum loss can be substantial even when covered or cash-secured. Time generally helps the seller as extrinsic value decays.
Important: "Covered" and "cash-secured" describe how the obligation is funded — they do not make a trade risk-free. Options can expire worthless, shares can fall sharply, and short options may be assigned early.
The three basic rules
  • Sell a covered call — own 100 shares, sell 1 call. Use when neutral to moderately bullish; pick a strike where you'd genuinely sell.
  • Sell a cash-secured put — reserve strike × 100 in cash, sell 1 put. Only on a stock you're willing to own, at a price you'd accept.
  • Buy a LEAPS call — pay a debit for 1+ year of upside without owning shares. Risk only premium you can lose completely.
Reading a payoff diagram
The chart in the Trade tab shows profit or loss at expiration across possible stock prices.
  • Horizontal axis: the stock price at expiration.
  • Green line: your total P/L for the position. Above the dashed zero line = profit.
  • Dots on the zero line: breakeven points — the prices where the trade neither wins nor loses.
  • Blue dashed line: the current stock price, for reference.
Key risks to respect
  • Assignment: a short option can be exercised against you any time before expiration — be ready to sell shares (covered call) or buy them (cash-secured put).
  • Time decay (theta): long options lose value every day; that same decay is what pays short sellers.
  • Capped upside: a covered call trades away gains above the strike for premium today.
  • Total loss of premium: a LEAPS call can expire worthless if the thesis is wrong or too early.
  • Delayed data: this app prices off ~15-minute delayed quotes — always confirm live prices before placing a trade.
